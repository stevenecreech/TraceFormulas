Use `$...$` for inline mathematics and `\[...\]` for displayed mathematics in the LaTeX manuscript. Use AMS theorem environments for mathematical statements and proofs: `definition`, `proposition`, `theorem`, `lemma`, `corollary`, `example`, `remark`, and `proof`. In other chapters, reserve subsection headings for broader topics rather than using them as statement labels. Preserve the mathematical statements and their links when changing presentation.

Use an upright sans-serif superscript for transpose: `$A^{\mathsf{T}}$` and `$(1,1)^{\mathsf{T}}$`. Apply this consistently across manuscripts, comparisons, and lecture scripts. This denotes ordinary transpose, with no complex conjugation.

Always identify the ambient space in inner-product and norm subscripts. For an $L^2$ space, include its domain and measure: `$\langle f,g\rangle_{L^2(X,\mu)}$` and `$\|f\|_{L^2(X,\mu)}$`. Define the measure and scalar field at first use; retain normalization explicitly when changing spaces. Use the inherited ambient product for a subspace when appropriate, and keep the notational index synchronized.

Maintain a notational index for each volume part as its manuscript develops. Add or revise entries alongside definitions and convention changes: record the symbol, meaning, scope or normalization, and a stable hyperlink to its definition. Distinguish overloaded symbols and include only notation actually used in the manuscript. Volume 1a's index is Appendix I; its LaTeX is the shared source for the PDF and website.
