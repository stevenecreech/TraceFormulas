Keep prose, source verification, Lean build/axiom audit, human correspondence review, and video status separate. Identify the prose and Lean versions each review concerns. Do not claim that file presence, a website build, comments, `sorry`, or an unproved project axiom constitutes formalization.

Steven's editorial direction is a reference book without exercises or exercise solutions. Use definitions, precise statements, complete proofs, and selected worked examples as exposition. Do not add problem sets or disguise them as examples. Keep Chapter 0 focused on elementary trace and kernel identities; develop the individual automorphic trace formulas in their own chapters instead of surveying them at length in the introduction. Apply Steven's requested outline revisions while preserving the identities of retained sections.

Give each proposition or theorem one main mathematical task. When a statement combines distinct constructions, classifications, formulas, or consequences, separate them into shorter statement–proof pairs in dependency order. Use a lemma for a preparatory result and a corollary for a direct consequence. Preserve complete arguments, make hypotheses explicit in each new statement, and link to earlier results instead of repeating their proofs. Keep tightly related parts together when they express one result. On splitting a result, preserve its semantic label for the appropriate retained claim and update other links, notation-index entries, and prose/Lean correspondence notes to the new destinations.

Use `$...$` for inline mathematics and `\[...\]` for unnumbered displayed mathematics in the LaTeX manuscript. Number formulas that are referenced later with `\begin{equation}\label{eq:semantic-name}...\end{equation}` and cite them using `\eqref{eq:semantic-name}` in prose. Use `aligned` inside `equation` for a calculation with several lines and one number. Prefer precise equation references to ambiguous phrases such as “the formula above”; leave routine uncited calculations unnumbered. Equation numbers have the form chapter.section.number and use a separate counter from theorems. Use AMS theorem environments for mathematical statements and proofs: `definition`, `proposition`, `theorem`, `lemma`, `corollary`, `example`, `remark`, and `proof`. Chapter 0 retains its three main sections, with continuous exposition and examples woven into each section; do not add subsection headings there. In other chapters, reserve subsection headings for broader topics rather than using them as statement labels. Give statements optional descriptive titles and place semantic labels directly inside their environments. Definitions and examples use upright text, theorem statements use the plain theorem style, and proofs have the usual closing symbol. Preserve the mathematical statements and their links when changing presentation.

Develop supporting subjects in appendices as they arise in the mathematics, including special integrals, special functions, transform identities, and analytic prerequisites. Extend an appropriate existing appendix or add a focused appendix when needed. At the point of use, state the required result and hypotheses and link to its complete derivation. Appendix sections follow the same prose/Lean/correspondence workflow, with reusable formal results in the shared library; avoid circular dependencies on the chapter they support.

Use an upright sans-serif superscript for transpose: `$A^{\mathsf{T}}$` and `$(1,1)^{\mathsf{T}}$`. Apply this consistently across manuscripts, comparisons, and lecture scripts. This denotes ordinary transpose, with no complex conjugation.

Use parenthesized matrices and column vectors consistently: `smallpmatrix` for inline mathematics and `pmatrix` in displays. The shared `tex/macros.tex` defines `smallpmatrix` for both the PDF and website. Homogeneous coordinates such as `[u:v]` retain their brackets; they are not column matrices.

Always identify the ambient space in inner-product and norm subscripts. For an $L^2$ space, include its domain and measure: `$\langle f,g\rangle_{L^2(X,\mu)}$` and `$\|f\|_{L^2(X,\mu)}$`. Define the measure and scalar field at first use; retain normalization explicitly when changing spaces. Use the inherited ambient product for a subspace when appropriate, and keep the notational index synchronized.

Keep the arguments of distances, invariants, and other functions explicit in statements and proofs: write `$d_{\mathbb H}(z,w)$` and `$u(z,w)$`. If a shorter symbol is useful, define it locally before using it, for example `$r=d_{\mathbb H}(i,w)$`.

When citing a numbered equation, include its established name or a short descriptive phrase alongside `\eqref`, for example “the inverse Cayley imaginary-part formula \eqref{eq:inverse-cayley-height}” or “the transformation law for gradients \eqref{eq:gradient-transport}”. The description identifies the mathematical role; the number locates the formula. Use natural descriptions rather than inventing formal names, and name each formula when combining several references.

Maintain a notational index for each volume part as its manuscript develops. Add or revise entries alongside definitions and convention changes: record the symbol, meaning, scope or normalization, and a stable hyperlink to its definition. Distinguish overloaded symbols and include only notation actually used in the manuscript. Volume 1a's index is Appendix I; its LaTeX is the shared source for the PDF and website.

Coordinate parallel work by section and file. Read current saved sources before editing and preserve changes made while another agent is working. Formalization can progress alongside editing during active work; do not claim an unattended file-watching or background formalization service exists.

Use semantic LaTeX labels for results and references; internal IDs belong in labels or metadata, not printed prose. In the shared manuscript use named `\hyperref[label]{readable text}` links to chapter IDs, section IDs, heading labels, labels immediately inside supported theorem environments, equations, or prose anchors written as `\phantomsection\label{semantic-id}`. Put a theorem label just after its opening command and optional title, before displays. Use the prose-anchor pair outside numbered environments when preserving a removed heading's destination. Numbered equation references via `\eqref` work in the PDF and website within a volume; ordinary numbered `\ref`, per-line `align` numbering, and cross-volume references are not supported by the website. See `AUTHORING.md` for the tested equation syntax. Never claim reference support beyond what has been implemented and tested.
