Why should a sum over eigenvalues equal a sum over geometric or arithmetic objects? A trace formula starts by finding a quantity that can be computed in two ways. The difficulty, and much of its usefulness, lies in making both computations precise.

We begin with a matrix. Adding its diagonal entries gives the same number as adding its eigenvalues with algebraic multiplicity. The diagonal entries depend on coordinates; their sum does not. This elementary example introduces the pattern that the later chapters will develop for operators built from modular forms and hyperbolic geometry.
